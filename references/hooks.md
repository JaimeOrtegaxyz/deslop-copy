# Hook Bank & the De-bait Guardrails

How to open a section/headline with tension — *keeping the credibility mechanics
of good hooks and rejecting the engagement-bait ones.* The central fact:

> A "viral hook" list and an "AI-slop" blocklist are **largely the same list.**
> The same devices that win comments on a feed make product copy read formulaic,
> AI-generated, and untrustworthy. De-slop governs the hook.

## Hook pattern bank

"Avoid for" flags the engagement-bait patterns that make real product/landing
copy *worse*.

| Pattern | Template | Example | Use / Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Specific-number / proof** | "[Precise number] [did X] so you don't have to." | "We tested 2,147 headlines over 14 months. Here's what won." | **Use everywhere** — the safest pattern; specificity = credibility. |
| **Problem-callout (PAS opener)** | "Still [painful manual thing] every [period]?" | "Still reconciling invoices by hand every Friday?" | **Use** for heroes, ads, cold email. Names a real pain you solve. |
| **Outcome-without-pain** | "[Desired outcome] without [the usual cost]." | "Ship on-brand decks without touching a slide." | **Use** — strongest landing headline shape; promise + objection-killer in one. |
| **Before→After (BAB)** | "[Old painful state] → [new state], via [product]." | "From 6-hour reports to a 5-minute export." | **Use** for case studies, testimonials, pricing. |
| **Authority / contrarian-with-reason** | "Most [advice] says X. After [credential], I do the opposite." | "Most onboarding asks for 12 fields. We ship with 2 — here's why retention went up." | **Use** *if you can back it.* Avoid as empty contrarianism. |
| **Stakes / cost-of-inaction** | "Your [asset] is losing [quantified thing] right now." | "Your trial users churn in the first 90 seconds — most teams never see it." | **Use** sparingly with a real number. Avoid vague fear ("you're losing money!"). |
| **Direct question (self-reflection)** | "What would change if [metric] [improved] by [period]?" | "What would you ship if reviews took an hour, not a week?" | **Use** for engagement/email. Mild bait risk if purely rhetorical. |
| **Command** | "Don't [action] until you [do this]." | "Don't pick a CRM until you've run this 2-minute check." | **Use** for ads/CTAs with authority. Avoid when you can't justify the imperative. |
| **"How to [X] in [time]"** | "How to [outcome] in [short specific time]." | "How to migrate your data in one afternoon." | **Use** — concrete and scannable. |
| **The mistake** | "The [small thing] that costs [audience] [big consequence]." | "The default setting that doubles your AWS bill." | **Use** if the mistake is real and you fix it. Bait when fabricated. |
| **Curiosity gap / open loop** | "There's a reason [X] — and it's not what you think." | "There's a reason your best email underperforms. It's the preheader." | ⚠️ **Bait-prone.** OK when the payoff is immediate; bad when the gap is the whole product. |
| **Pure cliffhanger / teaser** | "Here's the thing… / The catch? / Plot twist:" | "We grew 40%. But here's the kicker…" | ⚠️ **Avoid for product copy.** A top AI tell; reads instantly clickbait. |
| **Fake-vulnerability UGC** | "I wish someone had told me this sooner." | "Nobody warned me about [thing]…" | ⚠️ **Avoid** in B2B/product. Native to TikTok; on a landing page it reads inauthentic. |
| **Trend/format-jack** | "[Current meme/format] but for [your thing]." | "POV: your dashboard finally loads." | ⚠️ **Avoid** for durable copy — dates in weeks, dilutes positioning. |
| **Listicle inflation** | "[N] [things] that [outcome]." | "7 ways to cut churn." | ⚠️ **Use only if N items are genuinely parallel.** Inflated lists are a named AI tell. |
| **Negativity / rage hook** | "Stop [common practice]. It's ruining your [thing]." | "Stop A/B testing your buttons." | ⚠️ **Bait.** Use the *contrarian-with-reason* version, never rage-for-clicks. |

## Frameworks that matter

The durable few — everything else is a permutation:

- **PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution.** Landing heroes and ads: name the pain,
  raise the stakes, present the fix.
- **AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.** Long-form sales pages/emails;
  best for *unaware* audiences.
- **BAB — Before, After, Bridge.** Case studies, "why switch" sections; best for
  *solution-aware* buyers comparing options.
- **FAB — Features, Advantages, Benefits.** Pricing and feature blocks; forces
  every feature to earn a "so what."
- **4Ps — Promise, Picture, Proof, Push.** High-ticket / long-form offers where
  you must build belief before the ask.

The rule worth more than any framework count: **match the framework to the
reader's awareness level** (AIDA for unaware → BAB for solution-aware). Elite
copy hybridizes (PAS hook + FAB body + AIDA close).

## Power words — the honest take

**Keep:**
- **Concrete / sensory / specific:** numbers, named nouns, time spans
  ("$23,847", "in one afternoon", "2 fields"). Specificity is the only reliable
  "power" lever.
- **Strong CTA verbs tied to the payoff:** "Start free trial," "Get the
  checklist," "See it in action" — beats "Submit / Learn more / Get started."
- **Plain verbs over inflated ones:** "use" not "utilize," "help" not
  "facilitate," "has" not "boasts."

**Cut (they're the same words AI and slop-detectors flag):** revolutionary,
ultimate, unleash, unlock, game-changer, cutting-edge, next-generation,
seamless, robust, supercharge, effortless, transformative, world-class. They
signal *more*, deliver *less*. Stacking them makes copy read like AI *and* a 2014
buzzword brochure at once.

Rule: **clarity and specificity first; "power" never at the cost of either.**

## How "viral" technique backfires on real product copy

The viral genre optimizes for clicks/comments on a feed, not belief and
conversion on a page. Traps to reject:

1. **The hook-genre IS the AI-tell.** "The catch?", "Plot twist:", "Here's the
   thing", numbered-list inflation, rhetorical-question openers — a viral-hook
   skill and an anti-slop skill pull in opposite directions.
2. **Open loops overpromise → bounce.** Curiosity-gap headlines the page can't
   immediately pay off spike bounce and lower conversion. Clickbait buys a
   traffic spike at the cost of the relationship.
3. **Engagement-bait attracts the wrong traffic.** Rage/contrarian hooks pull
   comments from people with zero purchase intent — vanity metrics up,
   conversion down.
4. **Formulaic openers flatten differentiation.** When every competitor uses the
   same 18 templates, your hero reads as templated/AI and your positioning
   disappears.
5. **Borrowed UGC intimacy reads fake in B2B.** First-person vulnerability and
   trend-jacking are native to a creator's Reel; transplanted to a SaaS landing
   page they erode credibility.
6. **Power-word stacking = double slop.** Emotional-power-word lists are the same
   vocabulary readers flag as hype.
7. **Feed metrics ≠ page metrics.** Hooks tuned for watch-time/shares don't
   optimize for time-on-page, return visits, or LTV.
8. **Trend hooks date instantly.** A meme-format headline is stale in weeks;
   durable copy can't be rebuilt every cycle.

**Directive:** keep the *credibility* mechanics (specific numbers, real
problem-callout, outcome-without-pain, honest before→after); reject the
*engagement-bait* mechanics (cliffhanger teasers, fake vulnerability,
rage-contrarianism, listicle/power-word inflation, trend-jacking).
